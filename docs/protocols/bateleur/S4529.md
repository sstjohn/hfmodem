# STANAG 4529 (maritime, 1240 Hz)

`hfmodem.bateleur.s4529` sends and receives STANAG 4529: the single-tone
waveform for maritime HF links in 1240 Hz. It was written from the Joint
Interoperability Test Command's *STANAG 4529 Conformance Test Procedures*
(April 2004). Its paragraph 1.2 and appendix B restate the STANAG's
requirements one by one (reference numbers 1–17). The STANAG's own text was
not available. No other implementation was read, and nothing has been tested
against another sender.

## The waveform

| Part | Test procedures |
|---|---|
| 8-PSK at 1200 Bd on a sub-carrier selectable from 800 to 2400 Hz in 100 Hz steps, 1700 Hz by default; 99% of the power within 1240 Hz | 1.2 b, d |
| A frame of 256 symbols (213.3 ms): 80 symbols of 2-PSK sync, then three blocks of 32 data and 16 reference symbols and a last of 32 data | 1.2 e |
| Sync from x⁵ + x² + 1 loaded with 11010 at every frame, the first symbol its least significant bit | 1.2 f; the 80 bits printed in table 1.6 |
| The 176 reference and data symbols scrambled by an 8-phase sequence from x⁹ + x⁴ + 1 loaded with ones, three bits a symbol | 1.2 g, h |
| One, two or three bits a symbol at channel rates of 600, 1200 and 1800 bit/s | 1.2 c, tables 1.2–1.4 |
| Coding, interleaving, interleaver synchronization and message protocol as STANAG 4285 annex E for the next higher data rate; 32 rows, each delayed 48, 24 or 12 more (long, 20.48 s) or 4, 2 or 1 (short, 1.706 s); the flush for each | 1.2 j, table 1.5 |
| Uncoded channel rates of 600, 1200 and 1800 bit/s | 1.2 k |
| An offset of up to 37.5 Hz, drifting up to 3.5 Hz/s | 1.2 i |

Every part of that is STANAG 4285's, symbol for symbol. That covers the frame,
the sync sequence (the procedures' 80-bit pattern is 4285's), the scrambler,
the bit-to-symbol tables, and the coding, interleaving and flush of the 4285
mode at twice the rate: the procedures' flush counts are what 4285's give. So
a 4529 transmission here is a 4285 transmission at half speed, moved to the
4529 sub-carrier (`stretch`). The receiver brings the audio back to 4285's
time scale and carrier (`Squeeze`, which streams) and runs the 4285 receiver,
reporting offsets and times on the real scale.

## Modes

Modes are named by user rate and interleaver as for 4285: `1200L`, `1200S`,
`600L` … `75S`. Uncoded modes take the channel rate: `1800U`, `1200U`,
`600U`. A sub-carrier other than 1700 Hz goes after a slash: `600L/2000`.
Both ends set the mode beforehand (`MODE 4529 <mode>` on the host port;
`offair render|decode 4529 --mode`). On the station, a sub-carrier is keyed
only if the whole pulse (±660 Hz) lies in the span the station declares
(365–2985 Hz), which means 1100 to 2300 Hz.

## How it is tested

Against itself through `bateleur.sim`'s channels:

- **The procedures' own figures.** The 80-bit sync pattern; the flush counts
  for every rate and both interleavers; 99% of the power within 1240 Hz;
  every symbol twice 4285's length.
- **Round trips.** 1200S at 20 dB, 15 Hz off; 600L on 2000 Hz at 15 dB, 20 Hz
  off; 300S at 8 dB; 75S at 3 dB, 30 Hz off; 150L on CCIR poor at 12 dB;
  1800U at 25 dB.
- **Streaming.** The streaming receiver gives what the offline one gives.
- **On the station.** A message sent from the host port keys through the
  arbiter and decodes back, and a replayed transmission reaches the host
  port.

Its performance follows 4285's, scaled to half the symbol rate. The same
multipath delay spans half as many symbols, while a Doppler spread is twice
as large against the symbol rate. So 4529 on a channel is 4285 on one with
half its delays and twice its spread, as measured in [README.md](README.md).
